    What each level feels like

    RPE is how hard the run feels to you: breathing, legs and how much you'd like to stop. Don't think about pace, just effort. The talk test is the easiest check.

      How the plan changes with the run you choose. Easy and comfortable runs are run by effort, not pace: the effort should stay the same all the way, and if it creeps up (on hills, in the heat or late on) you slow down, or walk, to keep it there. A hard-but-controlled run settles at "comfortably hard", 6 to 7, and only builds a little. Only an all-out race reaches the top of the scale: there, effort rises steadily the whole way even at an even pace, peaking at the line. Feeling fresh early is normal and isn't a reason to go faster; runners who chase that feeling usually slow down later. In a race it's fine for effort to rise a little on climbs, then let your breathing settle over the top and use the descent to recover.
